## The headline

**Free cash stopped tracking reality the first time you sold anything.**

`handleSellSubmit` wrote the sale proceeds into the *manual* free-cash override:

```js
setSwingFreeCashInput(prev => (Math.max(0, parseFloat(prev||'0') + netProceeds)).toFixed(2));
```

But the segment ledger reads:

```js
const freeCash = manualInputStr !== '' ? parseFloat(manualInputStr) : Math.max(0, calculatedFreeCash);
```

So one sale makes that field non-empty — and from that moment `calculatedFreeCash`
is ignored **permanently**. Free cash no longer responds to buys, capital
injections, withdrawals or broker adjustments. It only ever goes up, by sale
proceeds. And `segmentNetWorth = freeCash + holdingsValue`, so your headline net
worth drifts further from the broker every single trade.

It was also double counting: `calculatedFreeCash` already adds `totalSellProceeds`
and subtracts `totalClosedCharges` from `soldHistory`, so recording the sale was
already the cash recycling.

**Fixed.** The sell handler no longer writes the override. The override now means
one thing only — *"this is what my broker actually shows"* — and is used to
reconcile against the ledger.

## Fixed

| # | Problem | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Sell handler latched the manual free-cash override (above) | Net worth silently diverged from broker cash after the first sale |
| **2** | `Math.max(0, calculatedFreeCash)` | A negative (overdrawn) cash position displayed as ₹0 — the exact signal reconciliation exists to catch |
| **3** | `liveLtps[sym] \|\| p.buyPrice` (2 sites) | A missing live price became the buy price, so unrealised P&L read exactly ₹0. A dead feed was indistinguishable from a flat position |
| **4** | Options `segmentNetWorth = openValuation + realizedPnl` | Every other segment uses `freeCash + holdingsValue`, where realised P&L already sits inside freeCash. Options added it on top, inflating the grand total by the whole realised figure |
| **5** | Options exit charges hardcoded `openCount * 40` | Ignored STT (0.15% of premium), exchange charge and GST. On a ₹2,00,000 premium the real figure is ~₹400, not ₹40 |
| **6** | Equity **options STT 0.1%** in `broker_profiles.js` | Zerodha's rate card says **0.15%** on the sell side. Every option in the Risk Desk was under-costed by a third |
| **7** | NSE exchange charge **0.00297%** in `journal_engine.js` | Correct figure is **0.00307%**. `broker_profiles.js` already had it right, so the same app priced the same trade two different ways |
| **8** | INDmoney equity exchange charge differed from Zerodha's | The exchange sets that fee, not the broker — they must be identical. A unit test actually *asserted they differ*, locking the error in |
| **9** | LTP polled every 20s, 24×7, six endpoints × 4s timeout | One cycle could take 24s and overlap the next; off-session it hammered the sources for a number that cannot change |
| **10** | No check that Swing % + LT % + Penny % = 100 | Anything logged against segment "ALL" is split by those percentages. If they don't total 100, every such entry creates or destroys money |
| **11** | Option premium never left any segment's cash | Cash committed to F&O vanished from the books |
| **12** | Closing an option credited the **full sale value** as new cash | A losing option round trip made the books richer |
| **13** | Buy / sell / capital-event / option-close handlers all wrote to the manual free-cash box | Each latched the override on and drifted from the ledger |

### New: reconciliation banner
Added at the top of the segment breakdown. For each segment it shows what the
ledger computes, what you entered, and the difference — plus a warning line for
each of: allocation ≠ 100%, empty capital ledger, and any holding with no live
price. This is the screen that answers "does my app agree with my broker".

## Two things you should know about your data

**Your capital ledger is empty.** `finplus_portfolio_backup.json` has
`capitalLedger: null`, `soldHistory: null`, `brokerAdjustments: null`,
`optionsTrades: null` — only 9 positions. With no injections recorded, the
calculated free cash has no opening balance, so it comes out negative (roughly
minus the cost of your holdings). That is now shown honestly rather than clamped
to ₹0, and the banner says why. **Log your capital injections and the calculated
figure becomes meaningful.**

**Your two apps disagreed on charges.** This app's rates were mostly right; the
paper-trade app's exchange charges were wrong in five places. I corrected that
app too (`charges.py` → engine `2026.09.2`): NSE equity 0.00297→0.00307%,
NFO options 0.05→0.03553%, NFO futures 0.0019→0.00183%, MCX futures
0.0026→0.0021%, MCX options 0.05→0.0418%. Both apps now match the live rate card.

## Options cash — fixed (added 10 Sep, after confirming Swing funds F&O)

Options are funded from Swing, so that is now modelled:

* An **open** option holds its premium out of Swing free cash.
* A **closed** option returns exactly its net P&L to Swing free cash.
* A trade may name its own source via `fundedBy`; anything unlabelled falls to SWING.
* The reconciliation banner reports how much cash is currently tied up in open premium.

While wiring this up I found a further bug in `handleCloseOptionSubmit`: closing an
option credited the **full sale value** (`exit × qty − charges`) to free cash, even
though the premium had never been deducted on the way in. A losing option round trip
therefore left the books *richer* than before the trade. That write is gone.

**Related — all automatic writes to the manual free-cash box are now removed.** The
buy handler, sell handler, capital-event handler and option-close handler each used
to mirror their event into that box. Every one of them latched the override on and
each was lossy in its own way (clamped at zero, ignored charges and adjustments, and
rounded the three shares of an "ALL" event separately so they no longer summed to the
amount entered). The box now means one thing: **the balance your broker actually
shows**. The app computes its own answer independently, and the banner shows the gap.
That separation is what makes an end-of-day reconciliation possible at all — if the
app maintains both numbers they can never disagree, and a real discrepancy stays
invisible.

## Not fixed — deliberate

1. **Still two charge engines.** `journal_engine.js` (3-pillar holdings) and
   `broker_profiles.js` (Risk Desk) remain separate. I centralised the rates inside
   `journal_engine.js` and corrected both, but a single shared module would stop
   them drifting again. `broker_profiles.js` is the better design — effective-dated
   and configurable — so it should absorb the other.
3. **`allow_origins=["*"]`** in `backend.py`. Safe today because every private
   endpoint requires the API key, but there is no reason for it to be `*`.
4. **`App.jsx` is 4,100 lines.** Every bug above lived in it. Splitting the segment
   ledger maths out of the component would make the next audit an hour instead of a day.

## Enhancements worth the effort

1. **EOD reconciliation log.** You already have the pieces — the banner computes the
   variance. Persist a daily row (date, ledger cash, broker cash, variance) so a
   drift shows up the day it starts, not a month later.
2. **Make the capital ledger mandatory before showing net worth.** Right now the app
   will happily display a confident number built on no opening balance.
3. **Per-position live price age.** Show "12s ago" under each LTP, like the paper-trade
   app now does, so stale prices are visible rather than inferred.
4. **XIRR, not just P&L.** With injections and withdrawals recorded, a money-weighted
   return is the number that actually tells you how the portfolio is doing.
5. **Corporate actions.** No handling of splits or bonuses. A 1:5 split turns a
   correct position into a 5× overstated loss overnight.
